The Sudden Gust that Knocks Your Hat Off was considered the lowest ranking official in the immortal Dragon King of the South Sea’s royal underwater court, but he was just happy to have gotten the job. There weren’t many places a minor wind demon could start over, you know. And he was on the way up, swear! Maybe a few thousand more years and he’d be out of the kitchens and somewhere a little less pressure-cooker. Somewhere a little more… scenic, like the gardens. The gardens would be nice. 
Today, the whole lagoon hummed with energy. The Dragon King of the South Sea’s Stormy Stew Soiree was the most anticipated event of the summer. It was a highly involved production that required hundreds of immortals across dozens of weeks to coordinate. There were broths to simmer, cumulonimbus clouds to condense, and storm paths to calculate. 

“You there! Wind demon.” 

The Unexpected Scalding Heat of a Ladle Handle was an imposing figure. Everyone had heard stories of the Head Chef boiling alive minor demons that dared to talk back, or dehydrating slackers down into bouillon cubes. He managed a quivering salute under her smoking gaze.

“Yes, chef?”

“How are the lowland breezes coming along?” 

“Um, the spice fields and cow herds are warmed–”

“Good, good. I have another task for you. You recently transferred from the Dragon King of the West Sea’s staff, correct? How would you feel about a trip back home?”

“But, chef—”

“No buts. This message is a special request from the Dragon King himself.” She bopped him on the head with a ladle and pointed insistently west. A scroll was dropped into his palm. “Now, off you go.”

“Yes, chef!”

Just his luck.

The beginnings of a tropical squall were brewing around the lagoon. Dark grey thunder clouds blotted out the sun, and the major storm demons were working overtime. The west sea was nearly two qilin’s leaps away, but Gust was surprised at how easily he could recall the journey. 

Taking the familiar air currents reminded Gust of a different time. A younger, sunnier time, when he was but two thousand and twenty. With the wind under his feet and warm air buoying them towards the heavens…

At the boundary between the south and west seas, where warm turquoise waters met darker blue in a distinct oceanic front, Gust descended until the waves lapped at his feet. Idly he watched as opposing gulfstream currents swirled against the border, distinct and unmixed. A school of herring, their silvery-blue scales catching in the sunlight, ducked and bobbed under the surface. He lifted a finger and plucked a straggler into a midair suspended bubble. Panicked, it flipped and flopped, its yellow eye darting wildly back and forth.

“My name is the Sudden Gust that Knocks Your Hat Off, and I am requesting an audience with the Dragon King of the West Sea. I am here on official business from the Dragon King of the South Sea’s Court.”

The fish gurgled. Satisfied, Gust released it.

He waited. The waves parted.

Then, he choked. The voice that arose from the depths was clipped and firm and monotone. But to Gust, it sounded like the most beautiful melody in the world. 

“My name is the Heat that Singes the Tip of Your Nose, and I am here to represent the Dragon King of the South Sea–”

“Nose?”

The great clam-shell Gate of the West Sea rose from the waves and hinged open. The Heat that Singes the Tip of Your Nose floated out, as striking and spectacular as the day he first laid eyes on her, ten thousand years ago. Her black eyes, dark as the void, met his, and he knew if he raised a single taloned finger he could feel the wonderful roughness on her rosy, scaled cheeks. 

“Gust.” Her voice crackled. 

Gust cleared his throat. “You’re looking well.”

“Same as you.”

“Um. How are things in the west sea?”

“Oh, you know. Same old. Stormy.”

“Good, good.” Gust shifted his weight. Even from a respectful distance, he could feel the rising heat of the fire demon warm his breezes, threatening to send him adrift. “I have an invitation. From His Auspicious Highness of the South Sea.” 

“An invitation…?” Nose raised an eyebrow. “You must know the Dragon King of the West Sea and the Dragon King of the South Sea are not on speaking terms.” 

This he did recall. 

“Maybe this is an apology,” he said. “An olive branch?”

“An olive branch?” The air simmered under Nose’s fiery blush.

“Maybe… the king wants to say he’s sorry. And that he’d love to see you. Um, him. And that he’s a different dragon now.” 

There was something impenetrable in her gaze.

“Maybe the king thinks it is a little late for apologies.” 

“Maybe the king thinks they are both young, and have more dancing to do.”

“Hmm.” The waves crested and fell between them. Gust lifted his chin, hopeful. “Perhaps the king will hear out this invitation.”

She vanished before the currents could notice her absence.

---

For two nights and two days the typhoon raged on. Inside the eye, among the gentle sloping reefs of the lagoon, the demons of the royal courts of the west and south seas danced and laughed and drank plum wine. It was a wonderful storm, and a delicious stew.

In a quiet moment, Gust stepped away from the sandbar. 

He felt the tip of his nose flush before he turned around. Her eyes flickered like embers when he held his hand out, and the heat of her palms lit something in his cheeks. A sea breeze picked up. Together they rose, hand in hand, arms interlinked, spinning and carried along by tempest winds.

They say on a hot summer day when the air is so thick you can swim in it, and the fever clamps down your lungs, you can see the dragon kings dancing in the clear sky like heat lightning, silvery-bright.
